The Setting

Costa Careyes, since 1968.

Founded by the Italian banker and artist Gian Franco Brignone, Careyes was conceived as a private community where architecture, art and the Pacific were meant to belong to one another. It remains family-owned and family-operated, reached by a road that keeps the outside world at a comfortable distance — roughly ninety minutes from Manzanillo International, and a short flight from Los Angeles.

Casitas de las Flores, designed by French architect Jean-Claude Galibert with Brignone, is the community's most recognised architectural gesture: saturated colour spilling down the hillside above Playa Rosa, within walking distance of the beach club that serves as the social centre of Careyes.

Ownership

Structure of the transfer.

Casita La Mandarina forms part of the Casitas de las Flores condominium regime within Costa Careyes. The transaction is structured as an assignment of rights (cesión de derechos) within that regime, subject to the community's own approval procedures.

Careyes sits within Mexico's restricted coastal zone, which means foreign buyers acquire through a bank trust (fideicomiso). This is standard along the entire Pacific coast and is handled by the notary and trustee bank as part of closing. Full documentation, community regulations and the complete transfer structure are shared with qualified buyers under confidentiality.
